Foundations

Reading rules before any frame

This surface is pre-edit review. It states what the catalog declares, what the repository resolved, what was not asked, and what could not be determined. It does not state confidence. No fixture exists yet, so every value in every frame is a placeholder slot.

Evidence vocabulary, exact words
Declared
asserted by DataHub metadata, or human-authored in the committed artifact
Resolved
mechanically computed by the producer
Not queried
we did not ask
Unresolved
we asked and could not determine

Coverage is stated as Complete against pinned manifest or Completeness not established. The word "verified" never appears. No percentage, no progress bar, no risk score.

Copy spec, inherited and corrected
  • workspace.json is always lowercase, even sentence-initial. Headings are sentence case.
  • No em dashes anywhere: colons, commas, parentheses instead. No emoji. No icon font.
  • RFC register: short, declarative, third person. Binary contrast framing (declared against resolved, DataHub-only against joined).
  • Paths, URNs, field names, counts and revisions are mono. Human-readable names lead; URNs and paths are copyable but secondary.
  • Attribution: the specification is maintained by Marcelle Labs under a single-steward model. No commercial product is named as author. The neutral producer is @workspacejson/cli@0.5.1, and the org is workspacejson, unhyphenated.
  • The artifact is not described as supplying per-file fragility or co-change clusters. It supplies what its disposition says it emits, and nothing more.
Encoding legend

Two independent axes. Source is who contributed the material. Resolution is what is known about it. They are never collapsed: DataHub material can be unresolved, workspace.json material can be declared.

Axis 1: source, text first
DataHubsquare corners, square pip, sky tint
workspace.jsonpill, round pip, emerald tint
Joinedhalf-square half-pill, diamond pip

Colour reinforces only. Shape and the literal word carry it, so the distinction survives greyscale, and workspace.json material never renders in DataHub's product blue.

Axis 2: resolution, equal weight
Declaredsolid outline, filled disc
Resolvedsolid outline plus 2px left rule, filled square
Unresolveddashed outline, open arc, same text weight and luminance as resolved
Not querieddotted outline, dotted ring, dimmed: absence of a question, not of an answer

Emerald is the brand accent, so it is withheld from resolution entirely. Resolution runs on the neutral ramp: resolved and unresolved sit at the same luminance and the same type weight, and only not queried is dimmed. Amber and red keep their meanings (experimental, error) and never touch unresolved. Unresolved is an honest terminal state, so it is stated, counted, and named.

Resolution accounting, the three states
complete renders as "Complete against pinned manifest, <total> of <total>". One line, no chart.
partial always names its residue. The count is never shown without the list, because a bare count is not renderable under the contract.
not-established shows its reason string verbatim, in the same position the count would occupy. The reason is never truncated or tucked behind disclosure.
Prohibited by construction: per-file fragility score, risk number, confidence percentage, progress bar. Nothing in the type contract can populate them.
Primitive map, and what is not in the list
Identity header: Card, Badge, Tooltip, Separator. Spine: Tabs. Rail nodes: Card plus Badge. Degraded banners: Alert. Accounting: Table. Residue and limitations: Collapsible. Inspect evidence: Sheet. Confirm and abandon: Dialog. In-flight: Skeleton.
Called out as outside the approved list: (1) the impact rail's connector lines are plain CSS borders inside a static grid, not a control and not a graph library; (2) the paired evaluation spread is a bounded inline SVG, not a chart component. Both are declared here rather than drawn as bespoke controls.
Why this comes first

The differentiator is honesty about coverage, so the vocabulary is the product. If a reviewer cannot tell "we did not ask" from "we asked and could not tell", every frame after this one is decoration.

The palette problem is solved by subtraction rather than by adding a hue. Emerald is the brand accent, and it was going to make resolved read as the good outcome everywhere it appeared. Resolution therefore runs entirely on the neutral ramp, distinguished by outline style and glyph, which leaves emerald free to mean "workspace.json contributed this" (a source fact, not a verdict) and leaves amber and red their existing meanings.

Open item for you: the Impact view's single decision. I have assumed it is "proceed to change plan, or stop", with coverage stated immediately beside the button rather than as a separate step. The alternative is making coverage itself the hero and demoting the action. See 1b and overrule me there.

Five-second hierarchy
  1. The dataset name, at 30px. A cold reviewer must know what is about to be edited before anything else.
  2. The coverage card, top right, at the same optical weight as the title. "Completeness not established" plus its reason string is the second thing read, deliberately ahead of any content.
  3. The two rail bands, labelled declared lineage and producer file plus code partners, so the joined contribution is legible as structure rather than as colour.
One decision

Continue, or stop. Both are buttons, and the caveat sentence sits above them so the accept-the-gap acknowledgement is unavoidable. Assumption flagged in 1a: if the reviewer's real job here is auditing coverage rather than gating, the right column becomes the hero and the buttons move to change plan.

Decisions worth noting
  • Unresolved and not queried have dedicated regions in the right rail, at body size. They are not tooltips and not a footnote.
  • The rail is a CSS grid with arrow glyphs. No force layout, no drag, no minimap: the comprehension gate for a node graph has not run.
  • Every node carries a source tag and a resolution tag as two separate chips. A DataHub node can read Unresolved, and a workspace.json node can read Declared.
  • Raw JSON and tool traces live only behind Inspect evidence, top right.

Why it looks like this

The failure mode to design out is a clean graph. An empty rail with tidy spacing reads as "no dependencies, safe to proceed", which is the opposite of what happened. So the empty slots keep their full size, take a dashed outline and a hatched fill, and carry a literal sentence: nothing declared, not the same as nothing upstream.

The connectors degrade from arrows to dotted stubs. There is no edge to draw, and drawing one would assert a relationship nobody returned.

Read state and completeness stay independent here: the read succeeded, the count is zero, and completeness is still not established. Zero observed edges never renders as proof of absence.

Why these four are compact

Each is local to one region and needs no new hierarchy: they reuse treatments already established, so a region frame is the honest amount of design. They are still designed states, not error handling: every one has a labelled treatment and copy that distinguishes what happened from what is true.

The rule they share

Red means something went wrong: rejected, and connection lost. Amber means unsettled: conflict, and revision mismatch. Dashed and open means we asked and could not tell. Dotted and dim means we did not ask. Nothing borrows another state's treatment, so the four words in the vocabulary stay learnable in one pass.
